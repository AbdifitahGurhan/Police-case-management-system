Blockchain-Based Digital Platform for Offense and Offender Tracking
CHAPTER I: INTRODUCTION
1.1 Background of Study 
Law enforcement institutions are fundamental pillars of any operation criminal justice system, as they are responsible for maintaining public order, preventing crime, and ensuring that justice is delivered fairly and with efficiency. One of the most critical responsibilities of police stations is the handling of criminal complaints, which involves the registration of reported offenses, investigation of suspicious, confirmation of evidence, and coordination with other justice-sector institutions such as the Criminal Investigation Department (CID) and enforcement offices. The effectiveness of these processes largely determines the quality of justice delivery within a society.
Globally, many law enforcement agencies have adopted digital case management systems to improve efficiency, transparency, and accountability. These systems allow police officers to register cases electronically, trail investigation progress, manage evidence securely, and share information across departments in a timely manner. Digitalization has proven to reduce administrative delays, minimize human error, and improve coordination among stakeholders involved in criminal investigations. As criminal human action become increasingly complex, the need for trustworthy and secure information management systems has become more desperate.
Across Africa, many countries continue to face challenges in modernizing their criminal justice systems, particularly in the area of case management and record keeping. Although some African nations have begun implementing digital systems within law enforcement institutions, a significant number of police departments still rely on manual, paper-based processes. These systems often lack integration, security, and interoperability between agencies. As crime becomes increasingly sophisticated and transnational across African regions, the need for secure, transparent, and technology-driven case management solutions has become more urgent. Digital transformation initiatives are therefore gaining attention as a means of strengthening institutional accountability and improving service delivery in the justice sector. 
In Somalia, primary police stations serve as the first point of contact between the public and the criminal justice system. Citizens report crimes such as thievery, rape, assault, homicide, and other offenses directly to these stations. As such, the manner in which police stations manage criminal complaints has a direct effect on public trust, crime control, and overall security. Despite this importance, many primary police stations in Somalia continue to rely on traditional paper-based record-keeping systems or partially implemented digital tools that lack integration and security.
Manual case management systems typically involve the use of physical record book, handwritten reports, and paper files stored in offices or archives. While these methods have been used for decades, they present significant challenges in modern law enforcement environments. Paper-based records are vulnerable to loss, damage, duplication, and unauthorized modification. Retrieving case information often requires manual searching, which is time-consuming and inefficient, particularly when handling with large amount of records or long-running investigations.
Another major challenge associated with traditional systems is the lack of transparency and accountability. In the absence of secure and traceable digital records, it becomes difficult to determine who accessed or modified case information and at what time. This can create opportunities for corruption, manipulation of evidence, or intentional delays in investigations. Such issues weaken the quality of law enforcement institutions and sabotage public confidence in the justice system.
The situation becomes even more captious in serious criminal cases such as homicide, organized crime, and violent offenses. These cases often involve multiple suspects, victims, witnesses, and different types of evidence, including forensic data, witness statements, and digital records. Managing such complex information using manual systems increases the risk of errors and inconsistencies, which can negatively affect investigation outcomes and judicial decisions.
In advanced information and communication technologies offer promising solutions to these challenges. Web-based systems provide centralized access to information, measurability, and ease of use, allowing authorized users to access case data from different locations. Such systems also support real-time updates, automatic reporting, and improved data organization. However, while web-based systems address accessibility and efficiency, they may still be defenseless to data change of state or unauthorized access if not properly secured.
Blockchain technology has appear as a powerful tool for enhancing data security and integrity in information systems. Blockchain is a decentralized and distributed record technology that ensures immutability, transparency, and traceability of recorded data. Once information is recorded on a blockchain, it cannot be adjusted without agreement, making it highly resistant to change of state. These characteristics make blockchain particularly suitable for sensitive applications such as criminal case management, where data integrity and trust are dominant.
By integrating blockchain technology with a web-based case management system, law enforcement agencies can ensure that criminal records, evidence logs, and investigation updates are stored securely and transparently. Such a system can provide role-based access control, ensuring that only authorized personnel can view or modify case information. Additionally, blockchain-based systems can support audit log, making it easier to track changes and identify accountability throughout the case lifecycle.
In the context of Somalia, the adoption of a blockchain-integrated web-based criminal case management system represents a important step toward modernizing law enforcement operations. It aligns with broader efforts toward digital transformation and institutional improvement while addressing long-standing challenges associated with manual record-keeping. This study therefore focuses on the design and implementation of such a system, with the aim of improving efficiency, transparency, and reliability in criminal case handling at primary police stations.
1.2 Problem Statement 
In an idealistic law enforcement environment, criminal complaints should be managed through a secure, efficient, and transparent digital system that supports accurate case registration, reliable evidence management, and seamless coordination among justice-sector institutions. 
An effective digital case management system would enable police officers to record criminal cases electronically, track investigations in real time, safeguard evidence integrity, and share information efficiently with CID units and prosecutorial offices. Such a system would improve accountability, reduce administrative delays, and strengthen public trust in the criminal justice system.
However, many primary police stations in Somalia continue to rely on outdated manual, paper-based systems. Criminal cases are recorded in physical logbooks, while supporting documents are stored separately in paper files. This fragmented approach makes it difficult to maintain accurate and consistent records throughout the investigation process.
Manual record-keeping creates significant challenges, including loss or damage of files, unauthorized alterations, and slow retrieval of information. The absence of centralized data limits the ability to track repeat offenders, analyze crime trends, and generate reliable reports for decision-making. These weaknesses can delay investigations, compromise evidence, and reduce public confidence in law enforcement institutions.
To defeat these challenges, there is a pressing need for a modern digital solution that addresses both efficiency and security concerns in criminal case management. This study suggest the design and implementation of a web-based digital case management system integrated with blockchain technology. The suggested system aims to provide tamper-proof storage of criminal records, improve case tracking, improve coordination among law enforcement institutions, and support more effective and transparent handling of criminal complaints at primary police stations in Somalia.
1.3 Research Objectives 
The main objective of this study is to design and implement a secure and efficient web-based criminal case management system integrated with blockchain technology for primary police stations in Somalia. 
The study aims to address the limitations of traditional manual record-keeping systems by introducing a digital solution that improves data security, transparency, and accountability in law enforcement operations.
The study also examine the current methods used in handling criminal complaints, identify challenges that reduce efficiency and reliability, and develop a system that supports accurate case registration and effective case tracking. 
The study aims to integrate blockchain technology to ensure the integrity of criminal records, implement role-based access control to protect sensitive information, and evaluate how the proposed system improves coordination, efficiency, and decision-making within the criminal justice system.
1.4 Research Questions
This study is guided by the following research questions:
How can a secure and efficient web-based criminal case management system be designed and implemented for primary police stations in Somalia?
How can a digital system address the limitations of traditional manual record-keeping practices in improving data security, transparency, and accountability within law enforcement operations?
What challenges exist in the current methods of handling criminal complaints, and how can a web-based system enhance accurate case registration and effective case tracking?
How can blockchain technology and role-based access control mechanisms ensure the integrity and security of criminal records while improving coordination, efficiency, and decision-making within the criminal justice system?
1.5 Motivation of Study
The motivation for this study arises from the growing need for efficient and transparent law enforcement systems in Somalia. Observed challenges such as delays, poor record management, and data inconsistencies in manual case handling processes highlight the urgent need for a modern digital solution. These challenges negatively affect the quality of investigations and reduce public trust in the criminal justice system.
From an academic perspective, this study is motivated by the opportunity to apply web-based technologies and blockchain concepts to solve real-world problems within the law enforcement sector. As a computer science student with an interest in secure systems and digital transformation, this research provides a practical platform to combine theoretical knowledge with system development while contributing to national efforts toward digital governance and institutional modernization.
1.6 Significance of Study
The significance of this study lies in its potential contributions to law enforcement institutions, the justice sector, and academic research. For primary police stations, the proposed system offers a structured and secure approach to managing criminal cases, reducing administrative workload and improving operational efficiency. For CID units and prosecutorial offices, access to reliable and consistent case data can support stronger investigations and prosecutions.
From a societal perspective, improved transparency and accountability in criminal case management can enhance public trust in law enforcement institutions and encourage citizens to report crimes. Academically, the study contributes to existing literature on the application of blockchain technology in law enforcement systems, particularly within developing country contexts such as Somalia. The findings may also serve as a reference for future research and system development initiatives.
1.7 Scope of Study
This study was conducted during the 2026–2027 academic year as part of the Bachelor of Computer Applications Final Year Project. It focuses on the design and implementation of a web-based criminal case management system integrated with blockchain technology for primary police stations in Somalia. The research examines existing criminal complaint handling procedures within the Somali law enforcement context and develops a prototype system tailored to improve case registration, tracking, and data security. The study is limited to the police station level and does not include court case management, sentencing processes, or correctional institutions.
