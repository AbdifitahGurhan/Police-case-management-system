CHAPTER II: LITERATURE REVIEW 
2.1 Introduction 
This chapter presents a comprehensive review of literature related to criminal case management 
systems, digital transformation in law enforcement institutions, web-based information systems, 
blockchain technology, and secure digital evidence management. The purpose of this chapter is 
to examine previous scholarly studies, technological developments, and practical 
implementations relevant to the proposed Police Station Management System (PSMS). 
The rapid advancement of Information and Communication Technology (ICT) has transformed 
the operational structures of organizations globally, including institutions within the criminal 
justice sector. Law enforcement agencies increasingly depend on digital technologies to improve 
operational efficiency, transparency, accountability, communication, and data security. 
Traditional paper-based methods are gradually being replaced by computerized systems capable 
of managing large volumes of sensitive criminal information effectively. 
Criminal case management systems play an important role in supporting police investigations, 
evidence handling, suspect tracking, and administrative coordination. Efficient criminal 
information management contributes significantly to justice administration, public trust, 
institutional accountability, and crime prevention. Poor management of criminal records may 
lead to delayed investigations, loss of evidence, information inconsistency, corruption, and weak 
coordination among security institutions. 
Modern policing environments require integrated digital platforms capable of supporting real-
time communication, centralized data management, and secure information sharing across 
administrative levels. As a result, many governments and security agencies have adopted web-
based management systems to automate criminal case registration, officer deployment, evidence 
tracking, and reporting activities. 
Recently, blockchain technology has emerged as one of the most promising innovations for 
improving digital security and data integrity. Blockchain provides decentralized and tamper-
resistant data storage mechanisms that help protect sensitive information from unauthorized 
modifications. In criminal justice environments, blockchain technology supports evidence 
protection, audit logging, chain of custody management, identity verification, and accountability 
mechanisms. 
Despite these technological advancements, many developing countries still face major challenges 
in implementing secure and integrated criminal case management systems. In Somalia, numerous 
police stations continue to rely on manual or semi-digital processes characterized by fragmented

2.2 Overview of Criminal Case Management 
Systems 
A Criminal Case Management System (CCMS) refers to a computerized platform designed to 
support the recording, tracking, monitoring, investigation, and reporting of criminal cases 
throughout their lifecycle. These systems are widely used by police departments, investigative 
agencies, courts, correctional institutions, and prosecutors to improve criminal justice operations. 
The primary objective of a CCMS is to improve operational efficiency while ensuring secure and 
accurate documentation of criminal investigations. Modern systems automate administrative 
activities and reduce reliance on manual paperwork. 
A standard criminal case management system commonly includes the following functionalities: 
 Criminal case registration 

--- PAGE 3 ---
3 | P a g e  
 
 Occurrence Book (OB) generation 
 Suspect management 
 Victim profiling 
 Witness statement recording 
 Evidence documentation 
 Investigation tracking 
 Officer assignment 
 Reporting and analytics 
 Access control 
 Audit logging 
 Case lifecycle monitoring 
Modern CCMS platforms typically rely on centralized databases and web-based interfaces that 
allow authorized personnel to retrieve and update information in real time. These systems 
support collaboration among departments and improve institutional coordination. 
Research indicates that digital criminal management systems significantly reduce record 
duplication, improve accessibility, minimize human errors, and strengthen accountability 
mechanisms. Electronic systems also improve crime analysis and strategic decision-making 
through automated reporting and statistical analysis. 
However, the effectiveness of a CCMS depends on several factors including: 
 System security 
 Infrastructure reliability 
 User training 
 Organizational readiness 
 Data integrity protection 
 Scalability 
Poorly designed systems remain vulnerable to unauthorized access, cyberattacks, and data 
tampering. Therefore, modern criminal management systems increasingly integrate cybersecurity 
technologies and cryptographic protection mechanisms. 
2.3 Traditional Manual Record-Keeping 
Systems in Law Enforcement 

--- PAGE 4 ---

Before the emergence of computerized systems, police institutions relied heavily on paper-based 
documentation to manage criminal investigations and administrative records. Police officers 
manually recorded incidents in physical registers, files, and archives. 
Although manual systems were initially manageable in small-scale environments, they became 
increasingly inefficient as criminal records expanded. 
2.3.1 Risk of Data Loss 
Paper-based records are vulnerable to: 
 Fire damage 
 Flooding 
 Theft 
 Physical deterioration 
 Human negligence 
Missing records can delay investigations and weaken institutional accountability. 
2.3.2 Slow Information Retrieval 
Manual archives require significant time and human effort to retrieve information. Officers often 
experience delays when searching historical case records or generating reports. 
2.3.3 Data Duplication and Inconsistency 
Repeated manual data entry frequently creates duplicate files and inconsistent information across 
departments. 
2.3.4 Weak Security Mechanisms 
Traditional systems often lack proper access control mechanisms. Unauthorized individuals may 
access confidential files, and documents can be altered without detection. 
2.3.5 Limited Coordination 
Manual systems make information sharing between police departments difficult, reducing 
operational collaboration and investigation effectiveness.

These challenges have encouraged governments worldwide to adopt digital transformation 
strategies aimed at modernizing law enforcement operations. 
2.4 Digital Transformation in Law 
Enforcement 
Digital transformation refers to the integration of digital technologies into organizational 
processes to improve operational efficiency, service delivery, communication, and decision-
making. 
In policing environments, digital transformation involves replacing manual procedures with 
electronic systems capable of supporting modern law enforcement activities. 
Over the past two decades, law enforcement institutions have adopted technologies such as: 
 Web-based management systems 
 Cloud computing 
 Geographic Information Systems (GIS) 
 Artificial intelligence 
 Digital forensics 
 Blockchain technology 
 Biometric authentication systems 
2.4.1 Improved Operational Efficiency 
Digital systems automate repetitive tasks and reduce paperwork, improving workflow 
management. 
2.4.2 Faster Communication 
Integrated platforms support real-time communication between officers, departments, 
prosecutors, and courts. 
2.4.3 Enhanced Transparency and Accountability 
Electronic systems maintain traceable records of all transactions and activities, reducing 
corruption risks.

2.4.4 Better Data Analytics 
Modern platforms generate crime statistics and reports that support evidence-based policing and 
strategic planning. 
2.4.5 Increased Accessibility 
Authorized users can access centralized databases remotely regardless of location. 
Despite these advantages, many developing countries continue to face challenges including: 
 Limited ICT infrastructure

 Organizational resistance to change 
2.5 Hierarchical Information Systems in Law 
Enforcement 
Hierarchical information systems are organizational systems where access permissions and 
operational visibility are structured according to administrative levels. 
The proposed Police Station Management System adopts a strict five-tier administrative 
hierarchy consisting of: 
1. State Administration 
2. Regional Administration 
3. City Administration 
4. District Administration 
5. Neighborhood/Police Station 
Hierarchical systems provide several advantages: 
2.5.1 Controlled Data Visibility 
Users can only access information within their administrative scope and subordinate levels. 

--- PAGE 7 ---
7 | P a g e  
 
2.5.2 Improved Security 
Sensitive criminal information remains compartmentalized, reducing unauthorized exposure. 
2.5.3 Better Officer Management 
Commanders can supervise officers assigned within their administrative jurisdictions. 
2.5.4 Efficient Organizational Coordination 
Hierarchical systems support structured communication and reporting channels. 
2.5.5 Accountability and Oversight 
Administrative supervisors can monitor activities occurring within their jurisdiction. 
Strict hierarchical separation is particularly important in criminal justice systems because it 
prevents unauthorized access to investigations and protects operational confidentiality.

2.8 Blockchain Technology 
Blockchain is a distributed ledger technology that stores records in linked cryptographic blocks. 
Each block contains: 
 Transaction data 
 Timestamp 
 Cryptographic hash 
 Previous block hash 
Blockchain possesses several important characteristics. 

--- PAGE 10 ---
10 | P a g e  
 
2.8.1 Decentralization 
Data is distributed across multiple nodes instead of a single centralized server. 
2.8.2 Immutability 
Recorded data cannot easily be altered without consensus. 
2.8.3 Transparency 
Transactions can be verified and audited. 
2.8.4 Security 
Cryptographic hashing protects records from tampering. 
2.8.5 Traceability 
Blockchain maintains permanent historical records. 
These characteristics make blockchain highly suitable for criminal justice systems where data 
integrity and accountability are essential.

2.16 Research Gap 
The reviewed literature demonstrates that digital criminal management systems improve 
operational efficiency, while blockchain technology enhances security, transparency, and 
integrity. 
However, several important gaps remain. 
First, many web-based criminal systems lack strong tamper-proof mechanisms capable of 
protecting sensitive records from unauthorized modifications. 
Second, blockchain-focused studies often remain conceptual without practical integration into 
daily police workflows. 
Third, existing systems rarely implement strict hierarchical visibility structures suitable for law 
enforcement institutions. 
Fourth, limited research addresses operational realities within developing countries, particularly 
Somalia. 
Finally, few systems integrate: 
 Criminal case tracking 
 Blockchain integrity verification 
 Officer deployment management
